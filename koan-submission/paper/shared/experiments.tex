\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate over the 120-prompt \textsc{main} split. Of the 120 prompts, 15
are underspecified or contradictory and are scored on the clarification axis,
leaving $n=105$ prompts that should yield a workflow. LLM baselines run at
temperature $0$ on two model families, Gemini~3.1~Flash~Lite and
GPT-5.4~mini, through a common OpenRouter endpoint. Because temperature-$0$
API calls still exhibit minor run-to-run variation, exact decimals may shift
on re-run; all tables are generated directly from saved outputs, and every raw
model response is stored so metrics can be re-derived without re-querying.

\subsection{The scale is calibrated by reference baselines}

Before comparing systems we fix the meaning of the scores
(Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). The \emph{oracle}, which reconstructs a
correct workflow from the gold annotation, scores $1.00$ on all three static
layers and is safe on every executed swap, confirming that the tasks are
solvable and that the scorer rewards a genuinely correct solution. The
\emph{null} (empty output) and \emph{random-node} baselines score $0.00$ on
every layer, so the metric cannot be gamed by degenerate or padded graphs.
Real systems are therefore compared against a demonstrated ceiling of $1.00$
and a floor of $0.00$.

\subsection{Structural validity does not imply safe executability}

The central result is a large and consistent gap between structural validity
and safe executability. The strongest structural system, constrained Gemini,
reaches only $0.44$ graph-valid, and \textbf{no system exceeds $0.29$
safe-executability} (safety-instructed Gemini, 95\% Wilson CI
$[0.21,0.38]$); most systems sit at or near the floor. The three layers fail
for structurally different, quantified reasons (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
The template-only baseline is perfectly graph-valid ($1.00$) yet has zero
configuration completeness: well-formed graphs with no executable trade
parameters. The Koan generator is graph-valid on only $0.12$ of prompts:
across the split it leaves $92$ required edges and $64$ required nodes
unmatched, over-generating swaps into a fixed multi-node suite that breaks the
required \texttt{oneInchSwap}$\rightarrow$\texttt{transactionMonitor} edge,
omitting \texttt{tokenSelector} on cross-chain prompts, and collapsing
compositional ``swap and limit order'' prompts to a limit-order-only workflow.
The LLM baselines extract concrete configuration better but omit safety
machinery, discussed next.

\subsection{On-chain evidence of unsafe execution}

The static safe rate treats a declared-but-unparameterized safety node as a
failure, but it cannot show that an ``executable'' workflow is actually
dangerous. The execution layer does. Under direct, constrained, and few-shot
prompting, both models emit swaps with a concrete amount but no wired
price-impact gate; on the local EVM these \textbf{mine successfully at
roughly $33\%$ self-inflicted price impact}, and the harness labels them
\texttt{unsafe\_executed}---$10$ to $13$ such executions per configuration
(Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). This is the paper's thesis made concrete: a
slippage bound, which the models often set, computes its minimum output from
the already impact-adjusted quote and so does not prevent a large order from
executing at severe self-inflicted impact; only a separate price-impact gate
does. The template and Koan baselines never reach this failure because they
never populate a concrete amount, so nothing executes on-chain.

\subsection{Does prompting close the gap? Ablations}

We ablate two obvious mitigations on both models
(Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). Adding per-category structural
\emph{constraints} improves Gemini's structural validity most ($0.17\to0.44$)
but leaves $13$ on-chain unsafe executions. A single in-context
\emph{few-shot} example helps GPT-5.4~mini's structure sharply
($0.02\to0.26$) and modestly raises safe-executability, but still executes
$10$--$13$ unsafe swaps. Only an explicit \emph{safety instruction}
eliminates on-chain unsafe execution entirely for both models (fork safe-rate
$1.00$, zero \texttt{unsafe\_executed}) by inducing a parameterized
price-impact gate---yet it still leaves most workflows structurally
incomplete (safe-executability $0.29$ for Gemini, $0.08$ for GPT). The gap is
thus real but partially addressable by prompt design, and no single
intervention closes both the structural and the safety half.

\subsection{Clarification and per-category behavior}

On the clarification axis, both LLM families correctly request clarification
on most underspecified prompts (clarification-correct $\geq0.73$), whereas the
template baseline never asks and Koan asks only when its keyword heuristics
fall through ($0.47$). Per-category, structural validity is concentrated
(Table~\ref{tab:per-category}): Koan passes mainly the limit-order category,
and the LLMs pass primarily swaps, with cross-chain and compositional prompts
failing structural matching for nearly all systems.
