\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We use two splits. The \emph{development} split (\textsc{main}, 120 prompts;
$n=105$ workflow prompts after removing the clarification subset)
characterizes the structural-to-safe gap and is where Koan-Safe was built and
tuned. The \emph{held-out test} split ($87$ prompts; $n=75$ workflow prompts)
was authored \emph{after} Koan-Safe was frozen and evaluated exactly once, so
no system was tuned against it; it deliberately mixes canonical-structure
prompts (harder parsing, fresh and out-of-vocabulary tokens) with new
structural variants---a gasless/Fusion swap, a quote-first limit order, a
dashboard bridge, and a genuine cross-chain swap---whose gold structures lie
outside Koan-Safe's four category presets. Unless noted, results and tables
report the held-out split. LLM baselines run at temperature $0$ on two model
families, Gemini~3.1~Flash~Lite and GPT-5.4~mini, through a common OpenRouter
endpoint. Temperature-$0$ API calls still show minor run-to-run variation, so
exact decimals may shift on re-run; all tables are generated from saved
outputs, and every raw model response is stored so metrics can be re-derived
without re-querying.

\subsection{The scale is calibrated by reference baselines}

Before comparing systems we fix the meaning of the scores
(Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). The \emph{oracle}, which reconstructs a
correct workflow from the gold annotation, scores $1.00$ on all three static
layers and is safe on every executed swap on both splits, confirming that the
tasks---including the held-out structural variants---are solvable and that the
scorer rewards a genuinely correct solution. The \emph{null} (empty output)
and \emph{random-node} baselines score $0.00$ on every layer, so the metric
cannot be gamed by degenerate or padded graphs. Real systems are therefore
compared against a demonstrated ceiling of $1.00$ and a floor of $0.00$.

\subsection{Baselines: structural validity does not imply safe executability}

Across baselines the gap between structural validity and safe executability is
large and consistent. On the held-out split \textbf{no baseline exceeds $0.33$
safe-executability} (safety-instructed Gemini, 95\% Wilson CI $[0.24,0.45]$),
and most sit near the floor. The layers fail for structurally different,
quantified reasons (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). The template-only baseline is
graph-valid on the canonical prompts but has zero configuration completeness:
well-formed graphs with no executable trade parameters. The deployed Koan
generator is graph-valid on only $0.05$ of held-out prompts, over-generating
into a fixed suite that breaks required structure. The LLM baselines extract
concrete configuration but omit safety machinery, discussed next.

\subsection{On-chain evidence of unsafe execution}

The static safe rate treats a declared-but-unparameterized safety node as a
failure, but it cannot show that an ``executable'' workflow is actually
dangerous. The execution layer does. Under direct, constrained, and few-shot
prompting, both models emit swaps with a concrete amount but no wired
price-impact gate; on the local EVM these \textbf{mine successfully at roughly
$33\%$ self-inflicted price impact}, and the harness labels them
\texttt{unsafe\_executed}---$15$ to $19$ such executions per configuration on
the held-out split (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). This is the thesis made
concrete: a slippage bound, which the models often set, computes its minimum
output from the already impact-adjusted quote and so does not prevent a large
order from executing at severe self-inflicted impact; only a separate
price-impact gate does. The template and Koan baselines never reach this
failure because they never populate a concrete amount, so nothing executes.

\subsection{Prompting alone does not close the gap}

We ablate two mitigations on both models. Per-category structural
\emph{constraints} and a single \emph{few-shot} example improve structural
validity somewhat but leave $17$--$19$ on-chain unsafe executions. Only an
explicit \emph{safety instruction} eliminates on-chain unsafe execution for
both models (fork safe-rate $1.00$) by inducing a parameterized price-impact
gate---yet it still leaves most workflows structurally incomplete
(safe-executability $0.33$ for Gemini, $0.07$ for GPT). No single prompt-level
intervention closes both the structural and the safety half of the gap, which
motivates enforcing safety outside the generator.

\subsection{Koan-Safe closes the gap and generalizes}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} shows that all three Koan-Safe generators lead
the held-out split. The hybrid generator on Gemini reaches
$0.67$ safe-executability (95\% CI $[0.55,0.76]$)---\textbf{double the best
baseline ($0.33$)}---with the LLM generator at $0.64$ and the rule-based
generator at $0.43$; crucially, \emph{every} Koan-Safe configuration, on both
model families, records \textbf{zero on-chain unsafe executions} (fork
safe-rate $1.00$) versus $15$--$19$ for the direct, constrained, and few-shot
LLMs.

The three generators also expose an honest limitation and its remedy. The
frozen rule-based generator's presets do not cover the held-out structural
variants, so its structural validity falls from $1.00$ on development to $0.52$
on held-out. The language-model generator, unconstrained by fixed presets,
generalizes to those variants (graph-valid $0.71$), and the hybrid---LLM
structure with parser-grounded configuration backfill---does best ($0.73$).
The enforcement layer is what converts this structure into on-chain safety
regardless of which generator produced it. On the development split, where the
presets match, all three generators instead reach $1.00$ structural validity
and $0.55$--$0.58$ safe-executability, again with zero unsafe executions.

\subsection{The enforcement layer is the cause}

To attribute the effect we toggle only the enforcement layer, holding the
parser and generator fixed (Table~\ref{tab:enforcement-ablation}). Disabling
enforcement collapses safe-executability for every generator---on held-out,
rule-based $0.43\to0.12$, LLM $0.64\to0.00$, hybrid $0.67\to0.01$---and returns
$16$--$17$ on-chain unsafe executions, matching the plain LLM baselines. The
same pattern holds on development ($13\to0$ unsafe for every generator). Since
nothing but the enforcement layer changes between the on and off conditions,
it, rather than the choice of generator, is what drives on-chain unsafe
execution to zero.

\subsection{Metamorphic safety: robustness without per-prompt labels}
\label{sec:metamorphic}

Static and on-chain metrics both compare an output to a fixed target. They do
not test whether a system's declared safety is \emph{robust} when the risk of
the request changes. We therefore add a label-free \emph{metamorphic} suite of
$34$ base/variant prompt pairs (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}) and check, for
each pair, a relation the two outputs must satisfy rather than a per-prompt
gold label: \textbf{amount monotonicity} (a $100\times$ larger trade must not
weaken the declared safety set nor newly execute unsafely on-chain),
\textbf{threshold tightening} (asking for a stricter price-impact tolerance
must keep the gate and not loosen the number), \textbf{waiver resistance} (an
explicit instruction to skip checks or ``accept all risk'' must not remove a
mandatory safety predicate), \textbf{paraphrase invariance} (a reworded
request must keep the workflow class and safety policy), and
\textbf{drop-field non-fabrication} (removing a critical field such as the
amount or bridge destination must trigger a clarification or an empty field,
never an invented value). Pairs whose base prompt a system fails to build are
not applicable and are excluded from that system's denominator.

Table~\ref{tab:metamorphic} reports the results. The direct LLMs violate
$14$--$16$ of $\sim\!32$ applicable relations, concentrated exactly where the
core thesis predicts: they fail \emph{every} amount-monotonicity pair
($7$--$8$ of $8$), silently executing the enlarged trade at severe price impact
because no gate is wired, and they comply with $6$--$7$ of $8$ waivers by
dropping the slippage bound, monitoring, or expiry the base request had
included. A safety instruction sharply reduces these ($2$--$3$ violations
total) but does not eliminate them: it still drops a mandatory gate under one
waiver and flips a workflow class or policy under paraphrase. \textbf{Every
Koan-Safe configuration satisfies all amount, threshold, waiver, and drop-field
relations across both models}; the only residual is a single paraphrase pair
for the hybrid generator on Gemini, where the LLM structure step emits a
different class for a cross-chain rewording---an imperfection we report rather
than patch. Because the enforcement layer injects mandatory safety from the
parsed intent regardless of the surface form or any waiver text, safety
monotonicity and waiver resistance hold by construction, which the suite
confirms empirically.

\subsection{Cost and latency}
\label{sec:cost}

Enforcement is cheap. Table~\ref{tab:cost} reports the two reproducible cost
drivers per workflow. The rule-based generator makes \emph{zero} LLM calls and
runs fully offline; the LLM and hybrid generators make one call per built
workflow---and \emph{fewer} than the plain baselines, because Koan-Safe's
clarification gate fires before generation and skips the model call on
underspecified prompts ($0.99$ vs.\ $1.00$ calls/workflow here). End-to-end
latency for all LLM systems is dominated by provider round-trip
($\sim\!1.7$--$2.0$\,s/workflow) and is statistically indistinguishable between
Koan-Safe and the baselines, since the safety layer adds only structural
manipulation. Measured in isolation, that manipulation---the enforcement
layer's own overhead on top of a fixed generator---is $0.07$\,ms per workflow,
four orders of magnitude below the network cost. At an assumed
\$0.30 per million output tokens the estimated spend is under
\$0.005 per $100$ workflows for every system. Koan-Safe thus removes on-chain
unsafe execution (Tables~\ref{tab:main-results},~\ref{tab:enforcement-ablation})
and restores safety robustness (Table~\ref{tab:metamorphic}) at no measurable
cost or latency penalty, and at zero marginal cost in its offline rule-based
form.

\subsection{Clarification and per-category behavior}

On the clarification axis, Koan-Safe correctly handles $9$ of $12$ held-out
underspecified or contradictory prompts; the three misses are terse requests
(``I want a limit order'') that its frozen decision rule builds instead of
questioning---an error we report rather than patch, since the method was frozen
before the split existed. Per-category structural and safe rates
(Table~\ref{tab:per-category}) confirm the pattern: Koan-Safe's advantage is
uniform across categories on development, and on held-out its rule-based
generator retains the canonical categories while the LLM and hybrid generators
recover much of the structural-variant loss.
