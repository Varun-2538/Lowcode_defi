\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Natural-language workflow generation is attractive for decentralized
finance (DeFi) because common user goals---token swaps, limit orders, and
cross-chain transfers---require coordinating multiple protocols and many
configuration choices. A system that turns ``swap 1 ETH to USDC with at
most 1\% slippage'' into an executable workflow could make DeFi accessible
to users who cannot write contract calls by hand.

Evaluations of such systems, however, typically stop at whether the
generated workflow, viewed as a directed graph of nodes and edges, is
structurally valid. We argue that this is the wrong bar for DeFi. A graph
that looks correct may still be unsafe or non-executable if it omits the
concrete trade parameters needed to run, ignores price-impact limits, uses
unsupported chains or tokens, or fails to gate execution against the cost
of the user's own order. In a domain where a single transaction can move
real funds irreversibly, structural validity is necessary but far from
sufficient.

This paper reframes DeFi workflow synthesis as a benchmark and measurement
problem centered on \emph{safe executability}. We evaluate generated
workflows on three nested static layers---structural validity, executable
configuration completeness, and declared safety---and, crucially, add an
execution layer that runs each generated swap on a real EVM and observes
the on-chain outcome. This lets us separate three questions that prior
evaluations conflate: does the graph have the right shape, does it carry
the parameters needed to execute, and, when executed, does it actually
behave safely?

Our first finding is that structural validity does not imply safe
executability, and the gap is large and consistent. Across the benchmark and
a range of systems---a deterministic template baseline, a deployed no-code
DeFi generator, and direct, constrained, few-shot, and safety-instructed
language-model baselines on two model families---no baseline exceeds $0.33$
safe-executability. On-chain, the direct, constrained, and few-shot
configurations each execute $15$--$19$ large swaps at roughly $33\%$
self-inflicted price impact because they set a slippage bound but no
price-impact gate.

The benchmark's failure taxonomy points to a fix. Because the dominant failure
is \emph{omitted safety machinery} rather than misread intent, safety should
be enforced on a candidate workflow rather than left to the generator. We
therefore build \textbf{Koan-Safe}, a system that factors synthesis into a
prompt-only intent parser, a replaceable candidate generator (rule-based, a
language model, or a hybrid), and a generator-agnostic safety-enforcement
layer that repairs structure and injects conservative safety policy---a
slippage bound, a parameterized price-impact gate, bridge confirmations---while
never fabricating trade intent it cannot read from the prompt. To test
generalization rather than memorization, we freeze the method and evaluate it
once on a held-out split authored afterward, which deliberately includes
workflow structures outside the method's presets. On that split Koan-Safe
reaches $0.67$ safe-executability---double the best baseline---and, across all
three generators and both model families, drives on-chain unsafe executions to
zero. An ablation that toggles only the enforcement layer, holding the parser
and generator fixed, moves unsafe executions from $16$--$17$ back to zero,
identifying the layer as the cause.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1) We introduce \textsc{DeFiFlowBench}, an
expert-authored benchmark over four DeFi task categories with difficulty
tiers, paraphrase clusters, and adversarial cases, a three-layer static
evaluation (structural, executable, safe) plus a clarification axis, and a
separate held-out test split with novel workflow structures for measuring
generalization. (2) We add an on-chain execution layer that runs generated
swaps on a real EVM and distinguishes a slippage bound from a price-impact
gate, turning ``declared safety'' into ``observed safety'', and we show the
static safety proxy is sound against this on-chain ground truth on both splits
(precision $1.00$, no false certifications). (3) We present Koan-Safe, a
generator-agnostic safety-enforcement layer, and show on the held-out split
that it doubles safe-executability over the best baseline and eliminates
on-chain unsafe execution across rule-based, language-model, and hybrid
generators; an enforcement on/off ablation isolates the layer as the cause.
(4) We add a label-free \emph{metamorphic} safety suite that checks robustness
relations---safety monotonicity under larger trades, resistance to explicit
safety waivers, invariance to paraphrase, and non-fabrication of dropped
fields---and show Koan-Safe satisfies them where the baselines fail every
larger-trade and most waiver pairs, at negligible enforcement cost
($0.07$\,ms/workflow and no extra model calls). (5) We calibrate the scale
with an oracle ceiling and null/random floors, report every rate with
confidence intervals, and provide a quantified failure taxonomy and robustness
analysis. (6) We release the prompts, gold annotations, baselines, the
Koan-Safe reference system, the execution harness, and analysis code for
reproducible evaluation.
